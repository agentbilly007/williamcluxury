Nevada's main down payment programs for Las Vegas and Henderson buyers are run by the Nevada Housing Division under the name Home Is Possible. You don't apply to the state directly. You apply through a participating lender. Here's what each program offers, from the program's own pages.

Who counts as a first-time buyer

Home Is Possible's first-time buyer program requires that you have not owned a home in the past 3 years. HUD uses the same 3-year test and says a married couple counts as first-time buyers if either spouse meets it.

Home Is Possible for First-Time Homebuyers

  • Down payment assistance of up to 4% of the total loan amount, usable for down payment and closing costs.
  • It is interest-free, and it is a 30-year, non-forgivable second loan.
  • Minimum credit score 640 (660 for manufactured homes), and a debt ratio of up to 50% depending on credit score and loan type.
  • You must live in the home as your primary residence and complete a homebuyer education course.
  • Clark County limits, active as of June 15, 2026: maximum purchase price $566,354; maximum household income $105,500 for 2 or fewer people and $121,325 for 3 or more. The program says certain areas may have a higher income limit.

Home Is Possible (no first-time requirement)

  • Up to 5% of the loan value toward down payment or closing costs.
  • No first-time buyer requirement. Qualifying income up to $165,000 and home price up to $832,750.
  • Minimum credit score 640 (660 for manufactured homes), and homebuyer education is required. Available statewide.

Programs for teachers and essential workers

  • HIP for Teachers: $7,500 for down payment and closing costs, forgivable after five years if you stay in the home, for licensed full-time K-12 public school classroom teachers in Nevada. The program lists it as available through December 31, 2026.
  • Worker Advantage: launched December 10, 2025 with $20,000 as a no-interest, no-payment, non-forgivable 30-year second loan for eligible healthcare, education, public safety and construction workers, first-come, first-served. Check the current price and income limits with a participating lender.

Homebuyer education

Every borrower on a Home Is Possible loan has to take a homebuyer education class before closing. The program uses a free online course.

Other programs you may hear about

  • Nevada Rural Housing (Home At Last, Launchpad): properties inside the city limits of Las Vegas, North Las Vegas and Henderson, and areas like Paradise, Enterprise, Spring Valley and Sunrise Manor, are not eligible. Winchester, Whitney and Summerlin South are listed as eligible.
  • Mortgage Credit Certificate: paused as of January 1, 2026 and not taking new reservations.
  • Clark County and the cities: Clark County's 2026-27 plan says Clark County and North Las Vegas do not currently run homebuyer assistance with HOME funds. The City of Henderson says it does not run a first-time buyer program and points buyers to Home Is Possible. Clark County also has a Community Land Trust for low-to-moderate income buyers, with entry by lottery.

Questions people ask

Does Nevada have a first-time home buyer program?

Yes. The Nevada Housing Division's Home Is Possible for First-Time Homebuyers offers down payment assistance of up to 4% of the loan amount, as an interest-free 30-year second loan, through participating lenders.

What are the income and price limits in Clark County?

For the first-time buyer program, limits active as of June 15, 2026 are a $566,354 maximum purchase price and $105,500 maximum household income for 2 or fewer people, or $121,325 for 3 or more.

What credit score do I need?

Home Is Possible lists a minimum credit score of 640 (660 for manufactured homes).

Do I have to be a first-time buyer?

Not for every program. Home Is Possible (up to 5%) has no first-time buyer requirement, with income up to $165,000 and price up to $832,750.

How do I apply?

Through a participating lender. You don't apply to the state directly.
