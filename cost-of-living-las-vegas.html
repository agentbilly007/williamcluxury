Living in Las Vegas costs less in taxes than many states: Nevada has no state personal income tax, and Clark County's sales tax is 8.375%. Housing is the biggest cost. Taxes

  • Income tax: Nevada has no state personal income tax (Nevada Department of Taxation). Federal income tax still applies.
  • Sales tax: 8.375% in Clark County, which includes Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas.
  • Property tax: Clark County charges a rate per $100 of assessed value, and assessed value is 35% of taxable value. 2026-27 rates: City of Las Vegas 3.2782, City of Henderson 2.9613, Summerlin Town and Spring Valley 2.9328, North Las Vegas 3.3544. Your own home's yearly increase is capped at 3% once you claim it as your primary residence. See Las Vegas property tax.
  • Estate tax: The Nevada Department of Taxation says Nevada does not require an estate tax filing for deaths on or after January 1, 2005.

This is general information. Taxes depend on your situation, so check with your tax professional.

HOA fees

Many homes here are in an HOA, and the fee changes a lot from one community to the next. Gated and guard-gated communities usually add more. There are also homes with no HOA, see no-HOA homes in Henderson. For any home you're serious about, I pull the current HOA fees and the resale package. In Nevada you can cancel within five days after you receive that package (NRS 116.4109).

Utilities

  • Power: NV Energy serves Las Vegas and Henderson. The normal high at the airport is 99.4°F in June, 104.5°F in July and 102.8°F in August, so expect your biggest bills in summer when the air conditioning runs. Ask the seller for the past 12 months of bills, or ask me to request them.
  • Gas: Southwest Gas, for homes with gas heat, water heaters, ranges or pool heaters.
  • Water: Las Vegas Valley Water District in most of Las Vegas and the unincorporated county; the City of Henderson and the City of North Las Vegas run their own water service. Pools and grass raise the bill.
  • Solar: Some homes have solar panels, owned or leased. A lease moves to the buyer only if you qualify and agree, so read the lease before you offer.

Rates change, so check the current rates with each provider. Links are in the sources.

Getting set up after you move

New residents get a Nevada driver's license and register their vehicles with the Nevada DMV, which lists the steps and fees for new residents. Budget for those along with movers and utility deposits.

Questions people ask

What is the cost of living in Las Vegas in 2026?

Housing is the biggest cost. From May 14 through November 10, 2026, the median single-family sale price across Henderson, Summerlin, northwest, southwest and North Las Vegas was $512,000 (Las Vegas REALTORS® MLS, not guaranteed). Nevada has no state income tax, and the Clark County sales tax is 8.375%. Summer power bills are the highest of the year.

Does Nevada have a state income tax?

No. Nevada has no state personal income tax. Federal income tax still applies. Check with your tax professional about your situation.

What is the sales tax in Las Vegas?

8.375% in Clark County, which includes Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas, according to the Nevada Department of Taxation.

What is the property tax rate in Las Vegas?

For 2026-27, the City of Las Vegas rate is 3.2782 per $100 of assessed value and Henderson's is 2.9613. Assessed value is 35% of taxable value, and a primary residence has a 3% yearly cap.

Are utilities expensive in Las Vegas?

Power bills peak in June through August, when the normal high is near or above 100°F. Ask for the home's past 12 months of bills before you buy, and check current rates with NV Energy, Southwest Gas and the water provider.
