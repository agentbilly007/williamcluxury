Relocating to Las Vegas
A Step-by-Step Guide for 2026

By William Crawford July 10, 2026 9 min read

Deciding to move to Las Vegas is the easy part. Pulling it off from another state — timing the home search, understanding the taxes, picking the right neighborhood sight-unseen, and closing without flying back and forth — is where most people get stuck. I relocate buyers into the valley every month, many of whom never set foot in their home until closing day. Here's the exact playbook I use, start to finish, so your move to Las Vegas goes smoothly in 2026.

First, the Why: What's Actually Pulling People Here

Before the how, it helps to know why the relocation numbers keep climbing. Nevada has no state income tax — nothing on wages, retirement income, or capital gains — which for most of my out-of-state clients is the single biggest financial reason to move. Stack that on a median valley home price around $440,000 (roughly half of comparable California and Pacific Northwest metros), 300+ days of sunshine, and no snow to shovel, and the math tends to make itself. If you want the full lifestyle picture, I break it all down in my Living in Las Vegas guide.

Your Relocation Timeline: The 90-Day View

Most successful relocations run on roughly a 60–90 day runway from "we're serious" to keys in hand. Here's how I sequence it.

Step 1 — Get Your Financing Squared Away (Weeks 1–2)

Before we look at a single listing, get pre-approved with a lender licensed in Nevada. An out-of-state pre-approval from your current bank often won't translate cleanly, and in a market where well-priced homes move fast, a local, verified pre-approval is what makes your offer competitive. If you're paying cash, we line up proof of funds instead. I can connect you with lenders I trust who specialize in relocation buyers — that relationship alone can save you weeks.

Step 2 — Nail Down Your Neighborhood (Weeks 2–4)

This is the step that makes or breaks a remote move. Las Vegas is enormous — more than 600 square miles across the valley — and the difference between loving and tolerating your move usually comes down to landing in the right community. Here's how I steer relocation clients:

  • Summerlin — the flagship master plan against Red Rock Canyon. Top schools, endless trails, Downtown Summerlin, luxury villages. My usual first recommendation for all-around quality of life. See the Summerlin guide.
  • Henderson — its own city to the southeast; clean, safe, and consistently ranked among America's best places to live. Green Valley, Seven Hills, Anthem, and Lake Las Vegas all live here. Full breakdown in my Henderson living guide.
  • Southwest & Mountain's Edge — newer construction and strong value with quick freeway access, without Summerlin pricing.
  • Strip-corridor high-rises — lock-and-leave, resort-style living for professionals who travel or want a second home.

We do this over video: I'll walk you through neighborhoods on FaceTime, send drive-through footage, and pull the trade-offs on schools, commute, and price so you can shortlist before you ever book a flight.

Step 3 — The Scouting Trip (Optional, Weeks 4–6)

Many buyers plan one focused two- or three-day trip once we've narrowed to a couple of neighborhoods. I structure it tightly: a block of home tours back-to-back, a drive through your top areas at different times of day, and stops at the everyday things that matter — grocery stores, gyms, your commute. Plenty of my clients skip this entirely and buy remotely; a good scouting trip just adds confidence.

Step 4 — Offer, Inspection & Closing — Remote-Friendly (Weeks 6–10)

Nevada makes out-of-state closings genuinely easy. Contracts are signed electronically, I attend the inspection on your behalf and send you a full video walkthrough with the inspector's findings, and closing can be handled by mail-away or a mobile notary wherever you are. You don't have to be in Las Vegas to buy a home in Las Vegas — you just need an agent who runs the process for you on the ground.

Don't Sell (or Break Your Lease) Before You Read This

Timing the two ends of a move is the most common stress point. If you own where you are now, we coordinate your sale and purchase so you're not carrying two payments or scrambling for temporary housing. If you rent, we back the timeline out from your lease end. Sometimes the right play is a short-term rental in your target neighborhood for 30–60 days so you can buy without pressure — I'll tell you honestly which approach fits your situation.

What to Budget For Beyond the Purchase Price

A relocation has costs a local move doesn't. Plan for long-distance movers (book 4–6 weeks out for the best rate), vehicle registration and a Nevada driver's license within your first weeks, and setting up utilities — NV Energy, Southwest Gas, and water through your local authority. Budget a little extra for summer cooling; it's a desert, so July and August air conditioning is your biggest utility line, offset by near-zero heating in winter. None of it is a surprise if you plan for it.

Getting Settled: Your First 30 Days

Once you're here, the setup list is short: update your address, register vehicles and get your Nevada license at the DMV (make an appointment — walk-ins are brutal), transfer or establish medical care, and if you have kids, confirm school zoning early since some of the highest-rated schools cluster in Summerlin and Henderson. Then comes the fun part — Red Rock Canyon and Mount Charleston within 45 minutes, Lake Mead, year-round golf, and world-class dining without tourist prices once you know where locals go.
