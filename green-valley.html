Green Valley is the middle of Henderson — on purpose

Green Valley was the first master-planned community in Henderson, laid out in the late 1970s and built through the 1980s and 1990s along Green Valley Parkway between Sunset Road and the 215. Everything that came after it in Henderson — Anthem, Seven Hills, Inspirada — was built further out and higher up. Green Valley stayed where it was: closest to the airport, closest to the Strip, and closest to the everyday things people actually drive to.

That is why it is the default answer for so many buyers. It is not the newest and it is not the most dramatic. It is the neighborhood with forty-year-old trees, the schools everyone has heard of, The District and Green Valley Ranch resort, the Henderson Multigenerational Center, and a fifteen-minute drive to the airport. Most of its homes were built between 1985 and 2005 and most of them trade between $400,000 and $600,000.

The communities, one by one

Green Valley North (89014). The oldest section, north of Warm Springs toward Sunset: 1980s ranch and two-story homes on established streets, many with no HOA or a very small one, and the lowest prices in Green Valley. Close to Sunset Station and the 215/I-515 interchange.

Green Valley central and Legacy (89014). Around Green Valley Parkway and the Legacy Golf Club: 1990s homes, more gated pockets, the Green Valley High School zone. Legacy's golf-course lots are where 89014's few homes over $600,000 tend to be.

Green Valley South and Pinnacle (89074). South of the 215 toward Silverado Ranch: newer (mid-1990s to 2000s), more gates, larger homes, and the Coronado High zone. This is where Green Valley's upper-middle market lives — 34 homes over $1 million on the day I pulled the data.

Green Valley Ranch (89052). The 1990s–2000s extension west of the 215 around The District and the GVR resort: bigger lots, more guard gates, and prices that run into Seven Hills territory. It shares its zip code with Seven Hills and Anthem, which is why the 89052 row below reads richer than Green Valley proper.

Arrowhead and Whitney Ranch (89014 edge). Smaller neighborhoods on Green Valley's north and east edges — good value, older housing stock, minutes from Sunset Park and the airport.

What Green Valley costs right now

In the two zip codes that are purely Green Valley — 89014 and 89074 — there were 307 single-family homes for sale when I pulled this, and 171 of them, more than half, were between $400,000 and $600,000. That is the Green Valley market in one number: it is the middle of Henderson. Only 18 homes were under $400,000 and 38 over $1 million, most of those in the gated pockets of Green Valley South. Green Valley Ranch sits in 89052, which it shares with Seven Hills and Anthem, so that row runs richer than GVR itself.

Drive times, honestly

  • Green Valley → Harry Reid International Airport: about 12–15 minutes — the shortest of any Henderson community
  • Green Valley → the Strip: about 15–20 minutes via I-215 and I-15 outside of rush hour
  • Green Valley → The District / Green Valley Ranch resort: 5–10 minutes from anywhere in Green Valley
  • Green Valley → Seven Hills and Anthem: 10 minutes south on the 215
  • Green Valley → Lake Mead: about 25 minutes

HOAs — what to expect

Green Valley is gentler on HOAs than the newer master plans. Many older streets in Green Valley North and central Green Valley pay only a small master association fee — some none at all — while the gated pockets, Legacy and Green Valley Ranch add a village HOA. On any home you are serious about I pull the exact monthly fee from the listing and read the resale package inside your due-diligence window, because Nevada gives you five days from receipt to walk if you do not like what is in it.

Schools

Green Valley is in the Clark County School District, which zones by street address. Green Valley High serves most of central Green Valley and the north; Coronado High serves Green Valley South and Green Valley Ranch. Two homes on opposite sides of the 215 can feed different schools, so I confirm the exact zoning for any address before you write an offer.

Who Green Valley is right for

  • Buyers who commute to the airport, the Strip or the resort corridor and want the shortest Henderson drive
  • Families who want established schools and parks without a new-construction premium
  • First-time and move-up buyers in the $400,000–$600,000 range — this is the deepest inventory in Henderson at that price
  • Buyers who want a mature neighborhood — real trees, finished streets, no construction next door
  • Investors — Green Valley rents fast and steadily

Questions I get about Green Valley

Green Valley or Green Valley Ranch?

Green Valley is the original plan — older homes, gentler HOAs, lower prices, closest to the airport. Green Valley Ranch is the newer extension around The District — bigger lots, more gates, higher prices. If your budget is under $600,000, you are almost certainly shopping Green Valley proper.

Is a 1990s home a problem?

Not in itself — it usually means a bigger lot and mature landscaping. What matters is what has been replaced: roof, HVAC, water heater, windows. That is exactly what the inspection is for, and in Green Valley it is where I spend the most attention.

How do you decide which part to show me?

Budget first, then whether you want a gate, then the school zone. Those three answers usually put you in one or two sections of Green Valley, and I send you the homes on video before you drive.
