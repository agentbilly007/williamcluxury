Living in Las Vegas
What to Know in 2026

By William Crawford June 20, 2026 8 min read

Most people picture the Strip when they think of Las Vegas. But the two million of us who actually live here barely go — we live in quiet master-planned suburbs, hike red-rock canyons on the weekend, and pay zero state income tax. If you're thinking about making the move, here's the real story on what living in Las Vegas is like in 2026, straight from a local agent who was raised here.

The Las Vegas Most People Never See

The Strip is about four miles of a valley that stretches across more than 600 square miles. Drive ten minutes in almost any direction and you're in neighborhoods that look nothing like the casinos — tree-lined streets, community pools, golf courses, and brand-new developments backing up to the mountains. For residents, the Strip is a place you take out-of-town guests, not where you live. The everyday Las Vegas is suburban, sunny, and surprisingly laid-back.

Cost of Living: The Honest Numbers

Las Vegas is genuinely affordable compared to the West Coast metros most of my clients are leaving. The median home price across the valley sits around $440,000, with master-planned areas like Summerlin and Henderson running higher. That's roughly half of comparable homes in Los Angeles, the Bay Area, San Diego, or Seattle.

The bigger story is taxes. Nevada has no state income tax — none on wages, none on retirement income, none on capital gains. For a relocating professional or retiree, that difference often covers a mortgage payment all by itself. Property taxes are also relatively low, and Nevada offers strong homestead protections. Groceries, utilities, and gas land near national averages; you'll spend more on summer air conditioning and less on heating.

The Best Areas to Live

Where you land depends entirely on your lifestyle and budget. Here's how I steer clients:

Summerlin

The valley's flagship master plan on the western edge, up against Red Rock Canyon. Top schools, Downtown Summerlin for shopping and dining, Las Vegas Ballpark, endless trails, and luxury villages like The Ridges. If you want the best all-around quality of life, this is usually my first recommendation. See my full Summerlin guide.

Henderson

Its own city to the southeast — clean, safe, master-planned, and consistently ranked among America's best places to live. Green Valley, Seven Hills, Anthem, Inspirada, and Lake Las Vegas all fall here. Great for families and retirees. I broke it all down in my Henderson living guide.

The Strip Corridor & High-Rises

If you want walkable, lock-and-leave, resort-style living, the high-rise condos near the Strip and in the southwest offer it — concierge service, skyline views, and zero yard work. Popular with second-home owners and professionals who travel.

Southwest & Mountain's Edge

Newer construction, strong value, and quick freeway access. A favorite for buyers who want a newer home without Summerlin pricing.

Spring Valley & Green Valley

Established, central, and well-priced — great launch pads for first-time buyers who want to be close to everything. See my Spring Valley and Green Valley guides.

Jobs & the Economy

Las Vegas is no longer a one-industry town. Hospitality and entertainment are still huge, but the valley has diversified into healthcare, logistics and distribution, professional sports, tech, and a growing professional-services sector. The arrival of major league sports — the Raiders, the Golden Knights, and more on the way — has accelerated investment across the valley. Remote workers love it here too: no income tax, lower cost of living, and an airport that connects nonstop to just about everywhere.

Schools

The valley is served by the Clark County School District, and like any large district, quality varies by zone. The good news for buyers: some of the highest-rated public schools, plus excellent charter and private options, are concentrated in the master-planned communities of Summerlin and Henderson. If schools matter to your family, build them into your search from day one — I'll help you target the right zoning before you commit to a neighborhood.

The Lifestyle: Sun, Trails & Everything Within Reach

This is the part that wins people over. More than 300 days of sunshine a year. Red Rock Canyon and Mount Charleston (with actual snow and skiing) both within 45 minutes. Lake Mead and golf year-round. World-class dining and entertainment any night of the week — without paying tourist prices once you know where locals go. And when you need to leave, Harry Reid International is one of the most convenient major airports in the country. It's a low-friction, high-amenity way to live.

What to Know Before You Move

A few honest realities: summers are hot — triple digits from June through September, so air conditioning is non-negotiable and you'll structure outdoor time around mornings and evenings. It's a desert, so landscaping is xeriscape, not lawns (which also keeps water bills and maintenance down). And the market moves fast on well-priced homes, so being pre-approved and ready matters. None of it is a dealbreaker — it's just worth knowing so the move goes smoothly.

Is Las Vegas a Good Place to Live?

For the vast majority of my clients relocating from higher-cost states, the answer is a clear yes — more home for the money, no state income tax, sunshine, and a genuinely good quality of life once you're settled into the right neighborhood. The key word is right. Las Vegas is enormous and varied, and the difference between loving and tolerating your move usually comes down to landing in the community that fits how you actually live.
