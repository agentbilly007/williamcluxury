Do I need a realtor
for new construction?

You don't have to have one. But the people at the model home work for the builder, and the timing of when you bring your own agent matters.

Start Your Move Plan Call or Text (725) 567-9482

By William Crawford, REALTOR® · Updated November 24, 2026

No, you don't need a realtor to buy new construction in Las Vegas, but the builder's sales team represents the builder, not you. If you want your own agent, bring them to your first visit, because builders set their own registration rules and may not accept an agent added later. William Crawford, a Las Vegas REALTOR®, represents buyers in new construction across Las Vegas and Henderson.

Who works for whom

  • The builder's sales team represents the builder. In Nevada, a licensee gives you a written disclosure of who they represent and the duties they owe (the Duties Owed by a Nevada Real Estate Licensee form).
  • Your own agent represents you, under a written buyer agreement that says what they do and how they're paid.

What your own agent does on a new build

  • Compares communities, plans, lot premiums and finished homes across builders, so you see more than one sales office's options.
  • Reads the builder's contract with you and points out the deposit terms, deadlines and what you can change. For legal questions, ask a Nevada real estate attorney.
  • Asks for incentives and price in writing, and helps you compare a builder's lender offer with others. I can share names of local lenders, and you choose.
  • Keeps track of the Nevada new-home deadlines, like the soils report request and rescission periods (NRS 113.135) and the public offering statement in an HOA community (NRS 116.4108).
  • Tours model homes and finished homes for you on video if you're out of state. See video home tours.
  • Gives your inspector access. A licensed home inspector you hire does the inspection and writes the report.

Registration: the step people miss

Builders decide whether and how they work with buyer's agents. They often require your agent to come with you, or register you, on your very first visit to the community, and some won't add an agent after you've been in. Before you walk into a model home or fill out a guest card, call your agent. I register with the builder before we go.

How a buyer's agent is paid

Your written buyer agreement sets how your agent is paid. Some builders offer to pay a buyer's agent, under their own policy, and the amount and terms vary by builder. It's negotiable. I explain how it works for the builders you're looking at before you sign anything.

When going alone can work

If you already know the builder, the plan and the price, and you're comfortable reading the contract and tracking the deadlines yourself, you can buy directly. Just know the sales team's job is to represent the builder.
