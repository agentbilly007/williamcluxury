Clark County property tax follows Nevada law, and the numbers are public. Here's how a bill is figured, what the rates are this year, and what changes when you buy a home.

How the bill is figured

Nevada assesses property at 35% of its taxable value (NRS 361.225). The tax rate is then charged per $100 of assessed value. The Clark County Assessor's own example: a $200,000 taxable value times 0.35 is $70,000 assessed value, and at the City of Las Vegas rate of 3.2782 per $100, the bill is $2,294.74 for the year, before any cap. Taxable value is set by the Assessor and is not the same as the price you pay.

2026-27 tax rates

  • City of Las Vegas (district 200): 3.2782 per $100 of assessed value.
  • City of Henderson (district 500): 2.9613.
  • Summerlin Town, Spring Valley, Paradise and some Enterprise districts: 2.9328.
  • North Las Vegas: 3.3544.

Clark County has 117 tax districts. Look up the exact rate for any district on the Clark County Treasurer's tax rate page.

The 3% cap on your primary residence

Nevada law caps the yearly increase in the tax bill on a single-family home that is the owner's primary residence at 3% (NRS 361.4723). Homes that are not owner-occupied get a higher cap, which is 8% in Clark County for 2026-27 (Nevada Department of Taxation). Only one property in Nevada can be your primary residence.

What happens when you buy

  • Clark County says any recorded ownership document removes the owner-occupied 3% cap, so a new owner has to claim it again. A refinance with no new ownership document doesn't affect it.
  • The Assessor mails a Tax Cap Abatement notice to owners who bought after July 1. Sign and return it by mail, online or in person. For 2026-27 you have until June 30, 2027 to correct your cap.
  • New construction doesn't qualify for a cap in its first fiscal year. It becomes eligible the next year.
  • The Treasurer mails bills only once a year, so if you buy during the year, call the Treasurer (702-455-4323, option 3) to get your bill.

When we look at a home together, I pull the current tax bill so you can see the real number, and I remind you to send in the primary residence notice after closing.

Due dates and penalties (2026-27)

  • Four installments: August 17, 2026; October 5, 2026; January 4, 2027; and March 1, 2027. Each has a 10-day grace period.
  • Late penalties under NRS 361.483 run from 4% for one late installment up to 7% of the full amount after the March due date.
  • If your loan has an escrow account for taxes, check your loan documents for how the payments are made.

Exemptions

The Clark County Assessor lists exemptions for veterans, disabled veterans, surviving spouses and blind persons, stated in assessed value. For example, the Assessor lists 3,640 in assessed value for a veteran and 36,400 for a 100% disabled veteran. Amounts change each year, and you file by June 15 before the fiscal year starts.

Appeals

If you think your taxable value is wrong, the Assessor's appeal forms are available in December, up to the January 15 filing deadline.

Questions people ask

What is the property tax rate in Las Vegas?

For 2026-27 the City of Las Vegas rate (district 200) is 3.2782 per $100 of assessed value, and assessed value is 35% of taxable value. Rates differ by district, so check the Clark County Treasurer's tax rate page.

What is the property tax rate in Henderson?

For 2026-27 the City of Henderson rate (district 500) is 2.9613 per $100 of assessed value.

Is there a cap on property tax increases in Nevada?

Yes. The yearly increase on the tax bill for an owner-occupied primary residence is capped at 3%. Other homes in Clark County have an 8% cap for 2026-27.

Does the 3% cap transfer when I buy a home?

Clark County says a recorded ownership document removes the 3% cap, so the new owner has to claim it again by returning the Assessor's Tax Cap Abatement notice.

When is property tax due in Clark County?

In four installments: for 2026-27, August 17, 2026, October 5, 2026, January 4, 2027 and March 1, 2027, each with a 10-day grace period.
