How buying new construction
works in Las Vegas.

Buying from a builder is different from buying a resale home: a different contract, different deadlines and different Nevada rules. Here's the order it usually goes in.

By William Crawford, REALTOR® · Updated November 24, 2026

Buying new construction in Las Vegas usually goes like this: bring your agent to the first visit, pick a community and a plan or a finished home, sign the builder's own purchase contract with a deposit, make design selections, then walk the home and close. Nevada gives you extra rights on a new home, including copies of construction-defect laws and soils report notices (NRS 113.135). William Crawford, a Las Vegas REALTOR®, represents buyers in new construction and tours model homes on video for buyers who aren't here yet.

Step 1: Bring your agent on the first visit

The sales team at the model home works for the builder. Builders set their own rules for registering a buyer's agent, and they often require your agent to come with you or register you at your first visit. If you walk in alone first, you may not be able to add an agent later. More on this in do I need a realtor for new construction.

Step 2: Choose a finished home or a build

  • Move-in ready or nearly finished homes (often called quick move-in or spec homes) are already built or close to it, with the finishes chosen.
  • To-be-built homes let you pick the lot, the plan and the finishes, and take longer.

The builder sets the price, the lot premiums and the timeline, and they can change. Get every price and promise in writing.

Step 3: Your loan, or proof of funds

Builders often offer incentives, and some are tied to using the builder's affiliated lender. You can choose any lender. Compare the full offer from more than one before you decide, and ask how long any rate buydown lasts. I can share names of local lenders, and you choose.

Step 4: The builder's contract and deposit

Builders usually use their own purchase contract, not the form used for resale homes. Read when your deposit becomes non-refundable, what happens if the build runs late, and what you can change after signing. For legal questions about the contract, ask a Nevada real estate attorney.

Step 5: Nevada's new-home rules

  • Construction-defect laws and soils reports (NRS 113.135). When you sign, the seller gives you copies of Nevada's construction-defect statutes and tells you about any soils report for the property or subdivision. If you ask for the reports in writing within 5 days of signing, the seller provides them at no cost within 5 days, and you may rescind within 20 days after you receive them. You can waive that right only in a signed writing.
  • Public offering statement (NRS 116.4102 and 116.4108). In an HOA community, the builder gives you a public offering statement by the time your offer becomes binding. Unless you have personally inspected the home, you may cancel by written notice until midnight of the fifth calendar day after you sign.

This is a general summary, not legal advice.

Step 6: Design selections

For a to-be-built home, you pick flooring, cabinets, counters and options at the builder's design studio. Upgrades can add a lot to the price and may need a larger deposit, so set a budget before your appointment.

Step 7: Construction, inspection and walkthroughs

Builders hold their own walkthroughs before closing. You can also hire a licensed home inspector, who does the inspection and writes the report. Check the contract for when the builder allows an inspection. I can give the inspector access and, with their OK, send you video.

Step 8: Closing, warranty and taxes

  • Read the builder's written warranty: what it covers, for how long, and how to file a claim.
  • New construction doesn't qualify for the 3% property tax cap in its first fiscal year (Clark County). See property tax.
  • Your timeline depends on your loan, your contract and the build.

Questions people ask

How does buying a new construction home work in Las Vegas?

Bring your agent on your first visit, choose a finished home or a build, line up your loan or proof of funds, sign the builder's purchase contract with a deposit, make design selections, then do walkthroughs and close. Nevada adds new-home rules, including construction-defect law copies and soils report notices (NRS 113.135).

Do I have to use the builder's lender?

No. You can choose any lender. Some builder incentives are tied to the builder's affiliated lender, so compare full offers. William Crawford can share names of local lenders, and you choose.

Can I cancel a new construction contract in Nevada?

In some cases. Under NRS 113.135, if you request soils reports in writing within 5 days of signing, you may rescind within 20 days after you receive them. In an HOA community, NRS 116.4108 lets you cancel within 5 calendar days of signing unless you personally inspected the home. This is a general summary, not legal advice; ask a Nevada real estate attorney.

Should I get a home inspection on a brand-new home?

You can. A licensed home inspector you hire does the inspection and writes the report. Check your builder contract for when inspections are allowed.
