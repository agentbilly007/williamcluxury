California and Nevada treat retirees very differently on income tax, and in different ways on property tax. This page compares the two in plain terms. It's general information, not tax or legal advice: check your own situation with your CPA, and ask an attorney about residency or estate questions.

After you move: a federal law, 4 U.S.C. 114, says a state may not tax the retirement income of someone who is not a resident of that state. It covers income from pensions, IRAs, 401(k) and 403(b) plans, government plans and certain other plans, with conditions for some payment types. Each state decides residency under its own rules, and California looks at where your real home is. Talk with your CPA about the timing of your move and your withdrawals.

Prop 13 vs Nevada's 3% cap

  • California: Prop 13 sets the base rate at 1% of assessed value plus voter-approved bonds and limits yearly assessment increases to 2%. A purchase generally resets the assessed value (California State Board of Equalization).
  • Prop 19 stays in California: homeowners 55 and older can transfer their tax base to a replacement home, but only to a home in California. It does not carry over to Nevada.
  • Nevada: the Assessor sets a taxable value, which is not the price you pay, and the assessed value is 35% of that (NRS 361.225). On your primary residence, the yearly increase in your tax bill is capped at 3% (NRS 361.4723). When you buy, you claim the cap again by returning the Assessor's notice.

The two systems aren't directly comparable, so look at the actual tax bill on each home. I show you the current bill on any home we look at. More detail: Las Vegas property tax.

Selling your California home

  • Federal exclusion: if you owned and lived in the home as your main home for at least two of the last five years, you may be able to exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 for many married couples filing jointly (IRS Publication 523).
  • California tax on the sale: California taxes capital gains as regular income. Gain from California real estate is California-source income even after you move.
  • Withholding at closing: California generally requires withholding of 3 1/3% of the sales price when you sell California real estate, unless an exemption applies, such as for a qualifying principal residence (Franchise Tax Board, Form 593).

Your CPA can tell you how this applies to you. For the California sale itself, work with a California-licensed agent there; I'm licensed in Nevada and handle the Las Vegas side.

Other differences to know

  • Sales tax: 8.375% in Clark County. California's statewide base rate is 7.25%, and local rates add to it (CDTFA).
  • Estate tax: neither state has its own estate tax today. Nevada's constitution also bans inheritance tax.
  • Medicare plans: if you move out of your Medicare Advantage or drug plan's service area, you can switch plans starting when you move and for two full months after (Medicare.gov). Check plans for your new ZIP code.
  • Driver's license: new Nevada residents must get a Nevada license within 30 days of becoming a resident (Nevada DMV).

Questions people ask

Does Nevada tax pensions and Social Security?

No. Nevada has no personal income tax, so there is no state income tax on Social Security, pensions or IRA and 401(k) withdrawals. Federal tax still applies. Check with your CPA.

Does California tax my pension after I move to Nevada?

Federal law (4 U.S.C. 114) bars a state from taxing the retirement income of a nonresident, which covers pensions, IRAs and most employer plans, with conditions for some payment types. California decides whether you are still a resident. Ask your CPA about your situation.

Can I take my Prop 13 tax base to Nevada?

No. Prop 19 base-year transfers apply only to a replacement home in California. In Nevada, the yearly increase in the tax bill on a primary residence is capped at 3%.

Do I pay California tax when I sell my California home?

Possibly. Gain on California real estate is California-source income. The federal home sale exclusion of up to $250,000 or $500,000 may apply, and California generally withholds 3 1/3% of the sales price unless an exemption applies. Check with your CPA.
